🌸 Safety First — Please Read Before Starting

🩺 Doctor Clearance

Always get clearance from your OB, midwife, or healthcare provider before starting any postpartum exercise program. Typically 6 weeks after vaginal birth, 8–12 weeks after C-section.

💜 Diastasis Recti Awareness

Avoid traditional crunches, sit-ups, or any exercise that causes your belly to "dome" or "cone." Focus on deep core breathing and gentle activation first. Consult a pelvic floor PT if unsure.

🌿 Pelvic Floor Considerations

If you experience leaking, heaviness, or pressure in your pelvis during any exercise, stop immediately. These are signs to reduce intensity and consult a pelvic floor physiotherapist.

🤱 Breastfeeding Note

Stay well-hydrated. Relaxin hormone remains elevated while breastfeeding, making joints more mobile and prone to injury. Avoid overstretching and prioritize stability exercises.

🛑 Stop Exercising Immediately If You Experience:

  • Increased vaginal bleeding
  • Pelvic pain or pressure
  • Chest pain or shortness of breath
  • Dizziness or lightheadedness
  • Urinary or bowel leakage
  • Abdominal "doming" or coning
  • Severe fatigue or pain
  • Incision pain (C-section)

Week 1

Reconnect & Breathe

This week is all about gently waking up your deep core and pelvic floor. Focus on breath, awareness, and very gentle movement. Rest is part of recovery — listen to your body above all else.

S1
Session 1 — Breath & Pelvic Floor
~15 minutes · No equipment · Low intensity
Diaphragmatic Breathing
3 sets × 10 breaths

Lie on your back with knees bent. Place one hand on your chest, one on your belly. Inhale slowly through your nose, letting your belly rise. Exhale fully through pursed lips, feeling your belly fall. This reconnects your diaphragm and pelvic floor.

Tip: Feel your pelvic floor gently descend on the inhale and lift on the exhale.
Pelvic Floor Activation (Kegels)
3 sets × 8 holds (3 sec each)

Lying on your back, gently contract your pelvic floor muscles as if stopping the flow of urine. Hold for 3 seconds, then fully release for 6 seconds. Focus on the release as much as the contraction — both are equally important.

Tip: Never hold your breath. Breathe normally throughout each hold.
Heel Slides
3 sets × 10 reps each side

Lie on your back, knees bent. Exhale and gently engage your core. Slowly slide one heel along the floor until your leg is straight, then slide it back. Keep your lower back in contact with the floor throughout the movement.

Tip: Exhale as you extend the leg. If your back arches, reduce the range of motion.
S2
Session 2 — Gentle Mobility
~15 minutes · No equipment · Low intensity
360° Rib Cage Breathing
3 sets × 8 breaths

Sit or lie comfortably. Place your hands around your lower rib cage. Breathe in and feel your ribs expand in all directions — front, sides, and back. Exhale and feel them gently compress. This activates the full core canister.

Tip: Imagine your rib cage as an umbrella opening on the inhale and closing on the exhale.
Cat-Cow Stretch
2 sets × 10 slow reps

On hands and knees, wrists under shoulders, knees under hips. Inhale and let your belly drop toward the floor (cow). Exhale and round your spine toward the ceiling (cat). Move slowly and mindfully, syncing breath with movement.

Tip: Move gently — this is a mobility exercise, not a stretch. Avoid any sharp pain.
Wall Push-Ups
2 sets × 10 reps

Stand facing a wall, hands at chest height. Step back so arms are nearly straight. Bend elbows to bring your chest toward the wall, keeping a straight line from head to heels. Exhale as you push back. Gentle upper body reactivation.

Tip: Engage your core gently throughout. Don't let your lower back arch.
S3
Session 3 — Gentle Walking & Core
~20 minutes · No equipment · Low intensity
Breathing with Core Engagement
3 sets × 10 breaths

Seated or lying down, practice coordinating your breath with a gentle core draw-in. On the exhale, gently draw your navel toward your spine without holding your breath. This is the foundation of all postpartum core work.

Tip: Think "gentle hug" not "suck in." The engagement should be subtle, about 30% effort.
Pelvic Floor Quick Flicks
3 sets × 10 quick pulses

Lying on your back, perform rapid, short contractions of your pelvic floor — contract and release quickly. These "quick flicks" train the fast-twitch pelvic floor fibers that respond to sudden pressure (coughing, sneezing, laughing).

Tip: Rest 30 seconds between sets. Quality over quantity — stop if you feel fatigue.
Heel Slides (Both Legs)
3 sets × 8 reps each side

Progress from Session 1 by adding a gentle pelvic floor contraction as you slide the heel out. Exhale and engage your core and pelvic floor simultaneously as the leg extends. This builds coordination between your deep core muscles.

Tip: If you feel any doming in your abdomen, reduce the range of motion.
🌿
Week 2

Activate & Stabilize

You've reconnected with your breath and pelvic floor. Now we add gentle stability exercises to begin rebuilding your core, glutes, and postural muscles. Move slowly and with intention.

S1
Session 1 — Core & Glute Activation
~20 minutes · No equipment · Low-moderate intensity
Bird Dog
3 sets × 8 reps each side

On hands and knees, engage your core. Slowly extend your right arm forward and left leg back, forming a straight line. Hold 2 seconds, return with control. Alternate sides. Builds deep core stability and back strength without spinal loading.

Tip: Keep your hips level — imagine balancing a glass of water on your lower back.
Glute Bridge
3 sets × 12 reps

Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Exhale and squeeze your glutes to lift your hips until your body forms a straight line from knees to shoulders. Hold 2 seconds at the top, then lower slowly. Strengthens glutes, hamstrings, and pelvic floor.

Tip: Drive through your heels, not your toes. Avoid overarching your lower back at the top.
Modified Plank (Knees Down)
3 sets × 15–20 sec hold

Start on forearms and knees, forming a straight line from knees to shoulders. Engage your core gently — no breath-holding. This pelvic-floor-friendly plank variation builds core endurance without excessive intra-abdominal pressure.

Tip: Stop immediately if you see any abdominal doming or feel pelvic pressure.
S2
Session 2 — Balance & Coordination
~20 minutes · No equipment · Low-moderate intensity
Standing Marches
3 sets × 20 steps (10 each leg)

Stand tall with feet hip-width apart. Slowly lift one knee to hip height, hold 1 second, lower with control. Alternate legs. Engages your core, hip flexors, and challenges single-leg balance — essential for functional postpartum recovery.

Tip: Hold a wall or chair for support if needed. Focus on slow, controlled movement.
Glute Bridge with March
3 sets × 8 reps each side

Perform a glute bridge, then at the top, slowly lift one knee toward your chest while maintaining hip height. Lower the foot, then lower your hips. This progression challenges pelvic stability and single-leg glute strength.

Tip: Keep your hips level when lifting the knee. Don't let the opposite hip drop.
Bird Dog with Elbow-to-Knee
3 sets × 8 reps each side

From the bird dog position, after extending arm and leg, slowly bring your elbow and knee together under your body (rounding slightly), then extend back out. This adds a gentle rotational core challenge while maintaining spinal stability.

Tip: Move slowly and with control. Breathe out as you bring elbow to knee.
S3
Session 3 — Full Body Gentle Circuit
~25 minutes · No equipment · Moderate intensity
Wall Push-Ups (Progression)
3 sets × 12 reps

Progress from Week 1 by stepping slightly further from the wall, increasing the challenge. Focus on a straight body line and controlled breathing. Builds upper body and core strength in a pelvic-floor-safe position.

Tip: Exhale on the push away from the wall. Keep your core gently engaged throughout.
Glute Bridge Isometric Hold
3 sets × 30 sec hold

Lift into a glute bridge and hold the position for 30 seconds, breathing normally throughout. This builds glute and hamstring endurance while training your core to maintain stability under sustained load.

Tip: Keep breathing! Never hold your breath during isometric holds.
Modified Plank (Extended Hold)
3 sets × 25–30 sec hold

Progress from Session 1 by extending your hold time. Focus on maintaining a neutral spine and breathing rhythmically. If you feel any pelvic pressure or doming, reduce the hold time and consult a pelvic floor PT.

Tip: Squeeze your glutes gently during the hold to reduce lower back strain.
🌸
Week 3

Strengthen & Mobilize

Your foundation is building. This week introduces functional movements like squats and side-lying exercises. Continue to prioritize breath coordination and pelvic floor awareness with every rep.

S1
Session 1 — Lower Body Strength
~25 minutes · No equipment · Moderate intensity
Bodyweight Squat
3 sets × 12 reps

Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, toes slightly turned out. Inhale as you sit back and down, keeping your chest up and knees tracking over toes. Exhale and squeeze your glutes as you stand. A fundamental functional movement for postpartum recovery.

Tip: Exhale on the way up. If you feel pelvic pressure, reduce depth and consult your provider.
Side-Lying Leg Lifts
3 sets × 15 reps each side

Lie on your side with legs stacked. Keeping your top leg straight, lift it to about 45 degrees, then lower with control. Targets the glute medius — a key muscle for pelvic stability, hip strength, and preventing lower back pain.

Tip: Keep your core engaged and avoid rolling your hips backward as you lift.
Cat-Cow with Hip Circles
2 sets × 10 reps each direction

From the cat-cow position, add gentle hip circles — slowly rotating your hips in a circle while maintaining a neutral spine. This improves hip mobility, relieves lower back tension, and gently warms up the entire core and pelvis.

Tip: Keep the circles small and controlled. Breathe continuously throughout.
S2
Session 2 — Upper Body & Core
~25 minutes · Optional resistance band · Moderate intensity
Bird Dog (Slow & Controlled)
3 sets × 10 reps each side

Progress from Week 2 by slowing down each rep — take 3 seconds to extend, hold 3 seconds, take 3 seconds to return. This time-under-tension approach significantly increases the core stability challenge without adding load.

Tip: Breathe throughout. Exhale on the extension, inhale on the return.
Standing Rows (Band or Towel)
3 sets × 12 reps

Anchor a resistance band or hold a towel around a door handle. Stand with slight knee bend, hinge slightly forward. Pull the band toward your lower ribs, squeezing your shoulder blades together. Strengthens the upper back and improves posture for nursing/carrying.

Tip: Keep your core engaged and avoid shrugging your shoulders. Exhale on the pull.
Modified Side Plank
3 sets × 20 sec each side

Lie on your side, propped on your forearm with knees bent. Lift your hips to form a straight line from knees to shoulders. Hold, breathing normally. Targets the obliques and lateral core without excessive pelvic floor pressure.

Tip: Stack your hips directly on top of each other. Don't let your top hip roll forward.
S3
Session 3 — Full Body Integration
~30 minutes · No equipment · Moderate intensity
Squat with Pause
3 sets × 10 reps

Perform a bodyweight squat, pausing for 2 seconds at the bottom before standing. This pause increases time under tension and improves hip mobility. Focus on keeping your heels down and chest tall throughout the movement.

Tip: Breathe in on the way down, exhale and engage your pelvic floor as you stand.
Standing March with Arm Swing
3 sets × 30 steps

Progress from Week 2 by adding an opposite arm swing as you march. This cross-body coordination pattern activates your deep core and mimics natural walking mechanics, preparing your body for more dynamic movement.

Tip: Keep your core gently engaged and stand tall. Don't lean to the side as you lift each knee.
Cat-Cow Cool Down Flow
2 sets × 12 slow reps

End your session with a slow, mindful cat-cow flow. Use this as a moving meditation — connect with your breath, release tension from your spine, and check in with how your body feels. A perfect way to close any postpartum workout.

Tip: There's no rush. Let each breath guide the movement. This is your recovery time.
🌻
Week 4

Build & Progress

You've built a solid foundation. This week introduces more challenging movements like lunges and hip hinges. Continue to listen to your body — this is a progression, not a race. Celebrate how far you've come!

S1
Session 1 — Strength & Power Foundation
~30 minutes · No equipment · Moderate-high intensity
Reverse Lunge
3 sets × 10 reps each leg

Stand tall, step one foot back and lower your back knee toward the floor. Keep your front knee over your ankle. Push through your front heel to return to standing. Reverse lunges are gentler on the knees than forward lunges and great for postpartum glute and quad strength.

Tip: Exhale as you push back to standing. Hold a wall for balance if needed.
Bodyweight Hip Hinge (Deadlift)
3 sets × 12 reps

Stand with feet hip-width apart. Push your hips back as you hinge forward, keeping a flat back and soft knees. Reach your hands toward your shins, feeling a stretch in your hamstrings. Drive through your heels and squeeze your glutes to stand. Builds posterior chain strength.

Tip: Think "hips back" not "bend forward." Keep your spine neutral throughout.
Plank Hold (Progress to Toes)
3 sets × 20–30 sec hold

If you've been symptom-free with the modified plank, try progressing to a full forearm plank on your toes. Maintain a straight line from head to heels, breathe normally, and stop immediately if you feel any pelvic pressure or see abdominal doming.

Tip: Only progress to toes if the modified version feels completely comfortable. There's no rush.
S2
Session 2 — Cardio Integration
~30 minutes · No equipment · Moderate-high intensity
Light Cardio Intervals (Marching)
4 rounds × 45 sec on / 30 sec rest

March in place with high knees and arm swings for 45 seconds, then rest for 30 seconds. This gentle cardio interval builds cardiovascular endurance without the impact of running. Listen to your body — reduce intensity if you feel any pelvic symptoms.

Tip: This is NOT the time to push through discomfort. Modify to slow marching if needed.
Squat with Pulse
3 sets × 10 reps + 5 pulses

Perform a bodyweight squat, then at the bottom, add 5 small pulses before standing. This increases time under tension and builds quad and glute endurance. Coordinate your breath — exhale on the way up from the pulses.

Tip: Keep your weight in your heels and your chest lifted throughout the pulses.
Bird Dog Circuit (Timed)
3 sets × 40 sec continuous

Perform bird dogs continuously for 40 seconds, alternating sides with a slow, controlled rhythm. This timed format builds core endurance and challenges your ability to maintain stability under fatigue — a key milestone in postpartum recovery.

Tip: Quality over speed. Slow down if your form breaks down.
S3
Session 3 — Full Body Strength Circuit
~35 minutes · No equipment · Moderate-high intensity
Alternating Reverse Lunges
3 sets × 12 reps (6 each leg)

Alternate legs with each reverse lunge, building a continuous rhythm. This challenges your balance, coordination, and cardiovascular system while strengthening your glutes, quads, and core. A great functional movement for daily life with a baby.

Tip: Exhale on each push-up to standing. Keep your torso upright throughout.
Hip Hinge with Arm Reach
3 sets × 12 reps

Combine the hip hinge with an overhead arm reach at the top of the movement. As you stand from the hinge, reach both arms overhead, then lower them as you hinge back. This full-body movement improves coordination and builds functional strength.

Tip: Keep your core engaged throughout. Breathe in on the hinge, out on the stand and reach.
Pelvic Floor Cool Down & Check-In
5 minutes · Lying down

End every workout — especially this final session — with a pelvic floor cool down. Lie on your back, perform 10 slow diaphragmatic breaths, then 5 gentle pelvic floor contractions and full releases. Check in with your body: any symptoms? Any discomfort? Celebrate your 4-week journey!

Tip: You've completed 4 weeks! Consider seeing a pelvic floor PT for a personalized assessment before progressing further.

🌸

You are doing an incredible job. Postpartum recovery is not linear — some days will feel harder than others, and that's completely normal.
